# Photography Brief

**Handloom saree e-commerce — product photography specification**
Version 1.0 · 5 August 2026 · Issued to: studio shortlist

---

## 0. Why this document exists first

Photography carries 60–70% of perceived quality in this category. A buyer deciding on a ₹50,000 handwoven saree cannot touch the silk, cannot tilt it to catch the zari, and cannot judge the drape. The photographs are the product until the parcel arrives.

It is also the longest-lead item in the project. Casting, studio booking, a test shoot, and a colour-approval round take six to eight weeks before a single production frame is shot. If this starts when the build finishes, launch slips a quarter.

Two rules follow, and they are the reason this brief is being written in week one:

1. **The shoot begins before the site is built, not after.**
2. **Nothing ships until colour is signed off against physical cloth.** A red that renders as orange loses the sale and earns the return.

## 2. Capture specification

### 2.1 Aspect ratios and resolution

| Frame type | Ratio | Delivered master | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-model portrait | 2:3 | **3000 × 4500 px** | Full drape needs vertical room; 2:3 is the category convention |
| Detail / flat | 1:1 | **3000 × 3000 px** | Macro on weave, zari, pallu, selvedge |

Capture must exceed delivery. Shoot full-frame or medium format at native resolution and crop to ratio in post — never crop in camera.

**Why 3000px and not 1600.** Category benchmarking found the majority of competitor masters sitting at 1440–1600px wide while the responsive image ladder advertises sizes up to 5000px — so the browser requests a size that does not exist and receives an upscale. On a ₹50,000 product, the buyer *will* pinch-zoom into the zari. At 1600px there is nothing there. At 3000px there is thread. This is the cheapest available quality differentiator in the entire project — it costs storage, not shoot days.

### 2.2 Technical minimums

- **Format:** 16-bit RAW, tethered capture. No in-camera JPEG as a source.
- **Sensor:** full-frame 45MP minimum; medium format preferred for detail frames.
- **Lens:** 85–135mm for on-model (no wide-angle distortion of drape); true macro (1:1) for detail frames.
- **ISO:** base ISO only. Noise reduction destroys weave texture.
- **Aperture:** f/8–f/11 on detail frames — the weave must be sharp corner to corner. Depth of field is not the place to be creative here.
- **Shutter:** strobe-synced. No continuous-light motion blur on fabric.
- **Working colour space:** ProPhoto RGB or Adobe RGB (1998) through the entire edit.

## 3. The shot list

Six frames per SKU, shot in this order. The order matters: the model is released after frame 5, and detail frames are shot with the cloth off the body.

### Frames 1–5 — on-model, 2:3 portrait

| # | Frame | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Full-length, front** | Entire drape head to hem, feet in frame, pallu visible. This is the grid thumbnail — it must read at 300px wide. |
| 2 | **Three-quarter, upper body** | Pallu detail, border across the shoulder, blouse visible |
| 3 | **Pallu feature** | The pallu is the most expensive part of the weave and the primary purchase driver. It gets its own frame. |
| 4 | **Movement or seated** | Drape behaviour — how the fabric falls, gathers, and holds. Georgette and Katan behave differently and the buyer needs to see which. |
| 5 | **Border / hem detail on body** | Border width and motif at wearing scale |

### Frames 6–7 — off-model, 1:1 square

| # | Frame | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | **Weave macro** | Fills frame with cloth. Raking light to raise thread relief. Zari must show metallic specular, not flat yellow. |
| 7 | **Blouse piece, flat** | Shot flat, evenly lit, true colour. Optional where no blouse piece is included. |

### Optional eighth frame

**Flat-lay full saree** (2:3), folded to show body + border + pallu together. Costly in floor space and time; useful for buyers comparing pieces side by side. Price it separately and decide after the pilot.

## 4. Styling and drape

**Engage a dedicated drape artist. This is not a stylist add-on.**

Drape is the bottleneck and the quality ceiling. A badly draped ₹1,00,000 saree photographs worse than a well-draped ₹20,000 one, and no retouching recovers it.

- **One consistent drape style across the catalogue.** Pick it at the test shoot and lock it. Inconsistent drape across a grid reads as amateur even when every individual frame is good.
- Nivi drape recommended as default — most legible, shows pallu and border cleanly.
- Pleats crisp, even, and counted. Pleat irregularity is the most common reason a frame gets rejected.
- Pallu pinned invisibly. Visible pins fail the frame.
- **Steam every piece before it goes on the model.** Fold creases are visible at 3000px and cost more to retouch than to steam.
- Blouse: a single neutral in 2–3 tones, reused across the catalogue. The blouse is not the product — it must not compete with the saree, and it must not date the imagery.
- Jewellery: minimal and consistent. It dates faster than anything else in the frame.

---

## 5. Lighting

Handloom silk with metallic zari is a specular-highlight problem, not an exposure problem.

**Requirements:**

- **Large soft key** — 6ft octabox or scrim minimum, close, high, slightly off-axis. Small sources make silk look like polyester.
- **Controlled specular for zari.** Zari is metal thread. It must produce a genuine metallic highlight that shifts as the eye moves across the weave. Flat, even lighting renders zari as beige paint and destroys the perceived value of the most expensive component in the cloth.
- **Do not cross-polarise.** Polarisation kills reflections — which is exactly the sparkle the buyer is paying for. This is the single most common lighting error in textile e-commerce.
- **Raking light on detail frames.** A hard, low-angle accent at roughly 15–30° to the cloth surface to raise thread relief and show the handloom texture that distinguishes it from powerloom.
- Separation light on model frames so dark sarees don't merge into the background.
- **Lock the lighting diagram at the test shoot and photograph it.** Every subsequent shoot day reproduces it exactly. Consistency across a 900-SKU grid matters more than any individual frame being marginally better.

## 6. Colour management — the non-negotiable section

Colour accuracy on silk is where this category fails, and returns are expensive when each piece is unique and unrepeatable.

**Mandatory process:**

1. **Colour target in the first frame of every setup.** X-Rite ColorChecker Passport or equivalent, shot under the exact production lighting. Every lighting change means a new target frame.
2. **Custom camera profile** built from the target, applied to the whole batch.
3. **Tethered capture to a hardware-calibrated display.** Calibration verified daily, not weekly.
4. **Physical swatch comparison at the shoot.** The actual cloth held beside the calibrated monitor under standard viewing light (D50/D65 booth). Sign off there, not from a laptop in an office three days later.
5. **Deliver in sRGB IEC61966-2.1** with the profile embedded, converted from the wide-gamut master. Never deliver untagged files.

**Known problem colours — flag at the test shoot:**

- **Saturated reds and magentas.** Routinely out of sRGB gamut. They clip to orange or lose all shadow detail on conversion. Bridal reds are the highest-value SKUs in the catalogue, so this failure mode hits the most expensive stock. Test the conversion path on a real red before production.
- **Golds and antique zari.** Drift warm-yellow or green depending on white balance. Zari should read as metal, not mustard.
- **Off-whites, ivories, and creams.** Collapse to plain white when exposed for the zari. The distinction between ivory, off-white, and cream is a purchase decision and it must survive to the browser.
- **Deep navies and blacks.** Lose weave detail in shadow. Expose for texture, recover in post.

---

## 7. Set and background

- **One background for the entire catalogue.** A warm neutral — bone, oyster, or soft stone. Not pure white (kills the fabric, reads as marketplace listing), not a colour that fights the product.
- Seamless, consistent across days. Note the exact paper or paint reference in the shoot bible.
- Detail frames: neutral surface, no props, no styling clutter. The weave is the subject.
- No seasonal or location sets in the core product shoot. Campaign imagery is a separate commission with a separate budget — do not entangle the two, or every campaign refresh obsoletes the catalogue.

---

## 8. Casting and model direction

- **One model for a whole collection wherever possible.** Consistency across a grid outweighs variety. A single face reads as a brand; five faces read as stock.
- Skin-tone range should reflect the actual customer base across collections, not within a single grid.
- **Buyout: perpetual, worldwide, all owned and paid digital.** Negotiate this up front. Renewable-term usage on catalogue imagery is a trap — the licence expires while the SKU is still live, and the piece is unrepeatable so you cannot reshoot it.
- Direction: still, composed, upright. The product is the subject. Editorial movement belongs in campaign work.
- Confirm hair and makeup consistency across days in writing. A visible change mid-collection is a reshoot the unique-stock model cannot absorb.

---

## 9. Post-production

**Per frame:**

- Dust, lint, loose thread removal
- Pin and clip removal
- Skin retouch: light and natural — blemish removal only, no reshaping, no skin smoothing that bleeds onto fabric edges
- **Fabric texture preserved absolutely.** No smoothing, no noise reduction, no clarity slider on the weave. If the retoucher cannot see individual threads at 100%, the frame is rejected.
- Background cleaned to consistent tone
- Crop to spec ratio
- Colour verified against the shoot-day target

**Explicitly forbidden:**

- Altering the colour of the cloth beyond neutral correction to the physical swatch
- Compositing pallu or border from another frame
- Adding zari sparkle in post
- Global sharpening that creates halos on thread edges

**Turnaround:** RAW selects within 48 hours of the shoot; retouched masters within 7 working days.

## 11. Throughput and scheduling

**Split the shoot into two day types.** This is the main cost lever in the whole brief.

| | Model days | Detail / flat days |
|---|---|---|
| Crew | Photographer, assistant, digital tech, drape artist, model, HMU | Photographer, assistant, drape/handler |
| Frames per SKU | 5 | 1–2 |
| Realistic throughput | **12–18 SKUs/day** | **40–60 SKUs/day** |
| Bottleneck | Drape (12–18 min per piece) | Handling and steaming |

Detail frames need neither model nor hair-and-makeup, so shooting them on separate days removes the two most expensive line items from roughly a third of the frame count. Studios that quote a single blended day rate for all frames are quoting you the model day for everything.

**Do not accept throughput promises above 20 SKUs/day on model days.** A studio quoting 30 has not draped a saree properly and is planning to reuse frames.

## 12. Budget model

Indian market, 2026. **These are planning estimates for sizing the line item — replace every number with an actual quote before committing.**

**Day rates:**

| Role | Range (₹/day) |
|---|---|
| Photographer + assistant | 35,000 – 90,000 |
| Studio with lighting | 10,000 – 25,000 |
| Digital tech / tethering | 8,000 – 15,000 |
| Drape artist | 8,000 – 15,000 |
| Model (e-comm, perpetual buyout) | 20,000 – 50,000 |
| Hair and makeup | 10,000 – 18,000 |
| **Model day, all-in** | **90,000 – 2,10,000** |
| **Detail day, all-in** | **55,000 – 1,30,000** |

**Post-production:** ₹120–350 per retouched frame.

**Derived per-SKU cost** at 15 SKUs on a model day and 50 on a detail day, 6 frames total:

> **≈ ₹7,500 – 18,000 per SKU, all-in.**

Multiply by your launch catalogue. For 300 SKUs that is roughly **₹22–54 lakh**. Compare that against the engineering budget before assuming the build is the expensive half of this project — in this category it usually is not.

**Levers, in order of effect:**

1. Cut to 5 frames (drop the second detail frame) — ~15% saving, modest quality cost
2. Batch detail days across multiple collections
3. Same-model multi-collection days — fewer buyouts, fewer HMU days
4. Own the studio space if monthly volume exceeds ~60 SKUs

**Levers to refuse:** cutting the drape artist, cutting the colour-management step, cutting master resolution.

## 13. Test shoot and approval gates

Do not commission production until a paid test shoot has cleared all four gates.

**Test shoot: 5 SKUs, chosen to break things.**

Include deliberately: one saturated red, one off-white or ivory, one heavy gold zari, one deep navy or black, one sheer georgette. These five cover every known failure mode.

| Gate | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **1 — Colour** | All five match physical cloth under D50/D65 viewing, verified after sRGB conversion, not before |
| **2 — Texture** | Individual threads resolvable at 100% on detail frames. Handloom irregularity visible, not retouched away. |
| **3 — Drape** | Pleats even, pallu correct, style consistent across all five |
| **4 — Grid** | All five frame-1 images side by side at 300px: consistent scale, crop, background tone, model position |

Gate 4 is the one studios fail. Individual frames pass; the grid looks like five different shoots. Check it before signing.

Only after all four clear: lock the lighting diagram, drape style, background reference, and crop template into a **shoot bible**, and photograph the set. Every subsequent day reproduces it.

## 14. What the studio must quote

Ask for line-item pricing, not a blended figure:

1. Model day rate, itemised by role
2. Detail day rate, itemised by role
3. Realistic SKUs per day for each type — with a named reference project at that rate
4. Post-production per frame
5. Model buyout: perpetual worldwide, all owned and paid digital
6. Test shoot, priced separately
7. Reshoot policy and cost for frames rejected against §13 criteria
8. RAW ownership and handover terms — confirm in writing that RAWs transfer to us
9. Turnaround commitment, with a stated penalty

**Also ask to see:** a full 6-frame set from a previous textile project, and a grid of 12 frame-1 images from a single collection. The second request is more revealing than the first, and most studios will not have one ready.

## 15. Acceptance criteria

A delivered SKU set is accepted only when all of the following hold:

- [ ] 6 frames delivered, correct types, correct order
- [ ] Portrait frames exactly 3000 × 4500; square frames exactly 3000 × 3000
- [ ] 16-bit TIFF, Adobe RGB, profile embedded
- [ ] Colour verified against physical cloth after sRGB conversion
- [ ] Weave texture resolvable at 100% — threads individually visible
- [ ] Zari reads as metal, with specular highlight
- [ ] No pins, clips, lint, or loose threads
- [ ] Background tone within tolerance of the reference
- [ ] Frame 1 legible and correctly scaled at 300px width
- [ ] Consistent with the shoot bible: lighting, drape, crop, model position
- [ ] RAW files delivered
- [ ] Filenames match the SKU convention exactly
